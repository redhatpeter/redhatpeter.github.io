I recently finished *Outliers* by Malcolm Gladwell, and it made me look at success from a different perspective.

We often think success is mainly about individual talent, hard work, and determination. Those are absolutely important—and we should always put in our best effort. But *Outliers* reminded me that success is rarely created by individual effort alone. Opportunity, environment, timing, culture, family, and the people around us can all play important roles.

Three thoughts stayed with me:

### 1. Are we in an environment where we can grow?

Sometimes it's not only about how hard we work, but whether we have the opportunities, support, circumstances, and environment that allow our efforts to turn into growth.

### 2. Are we helping create that environment for others?

This was probably my biggest reflection. We may not control every circumstance around us, but we can contribute to an environment where our colleagues, family, friends, and the next generation have better opportunities to succeed.

### 3. Know what you can and cannot control—and keep learning

We cannot control our background, timing, or every opportunity that comes our way. But understanding our circumstances, recognizing opportunities, and continuously building our knowledge can help us make better use of what we do have.

### My takeaway

> **My takeaway from Outliers: Success is personal effort multiplied by opportunity, environment, and the support around us.**

And perhaps an important question is not only, "What am I doing to succeed?" but also, "What am I doing to help create an environment where others can succeed?"
